# Best eSIM Options for International Travel: How to Choose Before Your Flight

An eSIM replaces the plastic SIM card with a digital profile downloaded onto a chip already built into the phone. For travelers, that removes the airport kiosk queue, the paperclip in the SIM tray and the risk of losing the home SIM in a hotel room. It does not remove the need to choose carefully. Two plans listed at the same price can perform very differently once you land: one rides on the strongest network in the country, the other on a partner with gaps outside the cities; one expires seven days after installation, the other only starts counting when it first connects abroad. Pricing structure is the factor travelers most often get wrong, because fixed bundles charge upfront for gigabytes that may never be used, while usage-based services such as [https://naked.net/de/en/](https://naked.net/de/en/) let a subscriber move from zero traffic to a large data allowance and pay at the end of each month only for what was actually consumed. This guide covers the five criteria that decide whether an eSIM works for a specific trip: coverage, pricing model, activation timing, customer support and keeping your home number reachable.

## Check Your Phone Before You Compare Plans

No plan comparison matters if the handset cannot use it. Three conditions must be met.

The phone needs an eUICC chip, the hardware that stores eSIM profiles. Dial `*#06#`: if the screen shows a 32-digit EID alongside the IMEI, the chip is present. Most iPhones from the XS and XR (2018) onward support eSIM, as do Google Pixel models from the Pixel 3 and Samsung Galaxy flagships from the S20 series. iPhone 14 and later models sold in the United States have no physical SIM tray at all. Versions sold in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau are an exception: many of them use two physical SIM slots and have no eSIM.

The phone must be unlocked. A device bought on contract may refuse any profile from another operator. On an iPhone, open Settings > General > About and look for "Carrier Lock: No SIM restrictions". On Android, the fastest test is to try installing a free or low-cost profile at home; a locked phone will reject it or show "SIM not supported".

The phone must handle two active lines if you plan to keep your home number running. This is called Dual SIM Dual Standby (DSDS). A phone can store several eSIM profiles (recent iPhones hold eight or more), but typically only two lines can be active at once, in any combination of physical SIM and eSIM.

## Coverage: Reading Beyond the Country List

A provider's website usually shows a list of countries and a flag. That list says nothing about signal quality. Three details tell you more.

### Which local network the plan uses

Most travel eSIMs are issued by an operator in one country and roam onto partner networks elsewhere. In a market with three or four operators, the partner might be the leader or the weakest of the group. In Japan, roaming plans typically connect to NTT Docomo, SoftBank or KDDI (au). In South Korea, the options are SK Telecom, KT and LG U+. In Thailand, the main networks are AIS and True. A provider that names the partner network on the product page lets you check that operator's coverage map for your actual route. A provider that only says "4G/5G available" leaves you guessing. Some plans allow the phone to switch between two or more local networks, which helps on road trips through mountains or rural regions where one operator has no towers.

### Technology generation

5G on roaming agreements is less common than the marketing suggests; many travel eSIMs advertise 5G but connect on 4G LTE in most countries. That is acceptable for maps, messaging and video calls. The real risk runs the other way: operators in the United States, much of Europe and parts of Asia have switched off 3G, and some are retiring 2G. A plan whose roaming agreement in a given country only covers older technologies may show signal bars and deliver no data.

### Routing and IP location

Roaming data is usually routed back through the issuing operator's core network before reaching the internet. This is called home routing. A traveler in Portugal using an eSIM issued by a Singaporean or Dutch operator may appear to websites as being in Singapore or the Netherlands. Latency rises by 50 to 200 milliseconds depending on distance, which is noticeable in video calls and online games. Banking apps, streaming services and some government portals may react to the foreign IP address with extra verification or geo-restrictions. Plans with local breakout send traffic directly to the internet from the visited country, which lowers latency and gives you a local IP address. Few providers state which method they use, so a pre-sale question to support is the only reliable way to find out.

### Regional plan exclusions

"Europe" plans often cover 30 to 40 countries but exclude Switzerland, the United Kingdom, Turkey, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina or Albania, depending on the provider's agreements. "Asia" plans may exclude China or bundle it with a separate surcharge. Check every country on the itinerary, including transit points where you might switch on the phone during a long layover. Cruise ships and aircraft use maritime and satellite networks that no standard travel eSIM covers.

## Pricing Models Compared

The headline price per gigabyte is only one part of the cost. Validity period, what happens to unused data, speed limits and top-up rules decide what you actually pay.

| Pricing model | How you pay | Best suited to | Main drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed data bundle | Upfront, for a set volume and validity (e.g., 10 GB for 30 days) | Trips with predictable usage | Unused data is forfeited at expiry |
| Daily or "unlimited" pass | Upfront per day or per period | Heavy users, hotspot needs, long video calls | Speed throttled after a daily threshold, often to 384 kbps–1 Mbps |
| Usage-based monthly billing | At the end of the month, for data actually consumed | Irregular travel, uncertain usage, frequent short trips | Requires monitoring to avoid an unexpectedly large bill |
| Home carrier roaming pass | Daily fee charged only on days the phone is used abroad | Short trips, travelers who need their home number for everything | Daily fees add up fast on trips longer than a week |
| Local prepaid eSIM from a destination operator | Upfront, at local prices | Stays of several weeks in one country | Registration may require a passport; not available in every country |

### Calculating the real cost per gigabyte

A 20 GB bundle for €30 looks cheaper than a 10 GB bundle for €20: €1.50 per gigabyte against €2.00. If the traveler uses 8 GB during the trip, the first plan costs €3.75 per gigabyte actually consumed and the second €2.50. Bundles reward accurate estimates and penalize caution. Usage-based billing reverses this: there is no penalty for overestimating, because nothing is paid for data that was never used.

### Top-ups and expiry rules

Read how top-ups work before buying. With some providers, a top-up adds data to the existing package and extends its validity. With others, it creates a separate package with its own expiry date, so leftover data from the first package disappears on schedule regardless of the top-up. Some plans reset the validity window with every purchase; others do not. A plan with 3 GB valid for 7 days looks cheap until you need to buy it three times on a 20-day trip.

### What "unlimited" actually means

Unlimited plans almost always include a fair-use threshold, typically between 1 GB and 5 GB of full-speed data per day. After that, speed drops to a level suitable for messaging and email but not for video. Hotspot sharing is often blocked or limited on these plans. If you intend to connect a laptop, confirm that tethering is permitted.

## Estimating How Much Data You Need

Most travelers overestimate. The figures below are typical consumption rates; actual numbers vary with app settings, video quality and background syncing.

| Activity | Approximate data use |
| --- | --- |
| Messaging (text, occasional photos) | 5–20 MB per day |
| Navigation with Google Maps or Apple Maps | 5–15 MB per hour of active navigation |
| Web browsing and email | 50–150 MB per hour |
| Social media feeds with autoplay video | 300–800 MB per hour |
| Voice calls over WhatsApp, FaceTime Audio or similar | 30–60 MB per hour |
| Video calls | 500 MB–1.5 GB per hour |
| Music streaming at standard quality | 40–150 MB per hour |
| Video streaming in HD | 1.5–3 GB per hour |

Downloading offline maps, playlists and films over hotel Wi-Fi before heading out cuts mobile consumption sharply. Turning off automatic photo backup to iCloud or Google Photos is the single most effective setting change: a day of sightseeing can produce 2 GB of photos and videos that the phone will try to upload over mobile data unless told otherwise.

## Activation Timing: When to Install and When the Clock Starts

Installation and activation are separate events, and confusing them costs travelers days of paid validity.

### Installing the profile

A travel eSIM is installed by scanning a QR code, by entering an SM-DP+ server address and activation code manually, or through the provider's app. Every method requires an internet connection. Install the profile at home, on stable Wi-Fi, one to three days before departure. Trying to do it in the arrivals hall on weak airport Wi-Fi with a queue of people behind you is how installations fail halfway through.

Many profiles can be installed only once. Deleting one from the phone, even by accident during a settings reset, can make it unusable, and the QR code will not work a second time. Some providers will reissue a profile on request; others treat a deleted profile as consumed. Keep the QR code and the manual codes in an email or a screenshot stored on another device, in case the reissue requires them.

### When validity begins

Providers use one of three starting points:

- **At purchase.** The validity period runs from the moment you pay, whether or not the profile is installed. Buy close to departure.
- **At installation.** The countdown begins as soon as the profile is added to the phone. Install the evening before the flight, not a week ahead.
- **At first connection in a supported country.** The plan stays dormant until the phone registers on a partner network. This is the safest model: you can install weeks in advance and lose nothing.

The terms of service state which model applies. If they do not, ask support before paying.

### Settings on arrival

Travel eSIMs need data roaming switched on for their own line, because technically they are roaming even in the countries they are sold for. The home line should have data roaming switched off to prevent charges. Set the travel eSIM as the line for mobile data. On an iPhone, also disable "Allow Cellular Data Switching", which otherwise lets the phone fall back to the home line's data when the travel line's signal is weak. Some profiles require a manual APN entry; the value is listed in the installation instructions and goes into Settings > Mobile Data > Mobile Data Network on iOS or Access Point Names on Android.

If the phone shows no service after landing, toggle airplane mode, wait two to three minutes, then switch network selection from automatic to manual and choose the partner operator by name.

## Customer Support: What to Check Before Buying

Support quality is invisible until something fails, and failures happen at the worst moments: at passport control, in a taxi with no Wi-Fi, late at night in a different time zone.

Look for live chat available around the clock, not an email form with a 48-hour response target. Check which languages the chat supports. A support team based in one time zone with business-hours staffing is of little use to someone stuck in an airport twelve hours ahead.

The provider's app should show remaining data in real time, allow top-ups in a few taps and display the expiry date. Without an app, you depend on SMS notifications that may not arrive, or on a web dashboard that needs data to load.

Read the refund policy. The common pattern: full refund if the profile was never installed, no refund once installed, and partial refund only if support confirms a technical fault on their side. A provider that documents partner networks, APN settings and the validity start rule on its public help pages generally handles problems better than one that hides these details behind a ticket.

A practical test costs nothing: send a pre-sale question, such as which network the plan uses in a specific country or whether hotspot is allowed, and note how long the answer takes and whether it addresses the question.

Plan for the paradox that support needs a connection. If the eSIM does not work, you need airport Wi-Fi, a café hotspot or your home carrier's roaming to reach the chat. Know which of these you will rely on before the flight.

## Keeping Your Home Number Active

Most travel eSIMs are data-only. They come without a local phone number, or with a number that only works for data services. Your home number therefore stays on its own SIM or eSIM, and the phone runs both lines side by side.

### Calls and SMS while roaming

Bank verification codes, two-factor authentication messages and delivery notifications arrive by SMS to the home number. To receive them, the home line must be registered on a network abroad, which requires roaming to be enabled at the carrier level, even with data roaming switched off on the phone. Some prepaid plans have international roaming blocked by default; request activation before departure.

Within the European Union and the European Economic Area, roaming regulation lets EU subscribers use calls, SMS and data in other member states at domestic rates, with fair-use limits on data. Outside that zone, incoming SMS is free with most carriers, but incoming calls are often charged per minute. Voicemail is a hidden cost: when a call is diverted to voicemail while you are roaming, some carriers bill it as two international legs, one to your phone and one back home to the voicemail server. Turning off conditional call forwarding before departure removes that risk. On many networks the code `##004#` cancels all conditional forwarding; your carrier can confirm the exact code.

### Messaging apps and Wi-Fi calling

WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram and iMessage stay tied to your home number even when the data comes from the travel eSIM. Contacts can reach you on those apps without any change. iMessage and FaceTime may ask you to confirm the number after switching lines; leave the home line enabled so registration stays valid.

Wi-Fi calling lets the home line make and receive normal calls over an internet connection, billed as if you were at home. Support depends on the carrier and the destination: some carriers allow it worldwide, others block it outside the home country, and some countries restrict it at network level. Test it before leaving: enable Wi-Fi calling, turn on airplane mode, reconnect to Wi-Fi only and place a call. If the status bar shows "Wi-Fi" next to the carrier name and the call connects, the feature works on your plan.

### Long trips and number expiry

Prepaid numbers in many countries are deactivated after six to twelve months without a top-up or outgoing activity. For long stays abroad, either schedule a small top-up, send one SMS per month, or move the number to a low-cost postpaid plan that keeps it alive. Losing the number means losing access to every account that uses it for verification.

## A Practical Scenario: Two Weeks in Japan and South Korea

A traveler based in Germany is flying to Tokyo for eight days, then to Seoul for six. The phone is an unlocked iPhone 15 with a physical German SIM. Typical daily use: one hour of navigation, an hour of social media, messaging throughout the day and two 30-minute video calls home per week.

1. **Confirm the device.** Dialing `#06#` shows an EID, and Settings > General > About shows no carrier restrictions. The phone can run the German SIM and a travel eSIM together.
2. **Estimate the data.** Navigation uses about 0.15 GB over the trip, social media about 0.6 GB per day (8.4 GB in total), messaging 0.2 GB and four video calls about 3 GB. With a margin, the realistic total is 12 to 13 GB.
3. **Compare the options.** A regional Asia plan covering both countries removes the need for a second installation in Seoul. Two separate country plans may be cheaper per gigabyte but involve two installations and two expiry dates. An unlimited plan is unnecessary at this level of use. A usage-based plan avoids the risk of paying for 20 GB and using 12.
4. **Check the networks.** The regional plan's product page lists SoftBank in Japan and KT in South Korea. Both have strong urban coverage; the itinerary includes a day trip to Nikko, which is covered by SoftBank's map.
5. **Buy and install.** The terms state that validity starts on first connection in a supported country. The traveler buys three days before departure, installs the profile at home over Wi-Fi and saves the QR code in an email.
6. **Configure the lines.** The German SIM is labeled "Home", the eSIM "Travel". Mobile data is assigned to "Travel", data roaming on "Home" is switched off, "Allow Cellular Data Switching" is disabled and voicemail forwarding is cancelled with the carrier.
7. **Connect on arrival.** At Haneda, the traveler turns off airplane mode, enables data roaming on the travel line and waits two minutes. The phone registers on SoftBank. A speed test shows 40 Mbps.
8. **Monitor and finish.** On day ten, the provider's app shows 5 GB remaining, enough for Seoul. After returning home, the traveler removes the expired profile and switches mobile data back to the German SIM.

The German number received two bank SMS codes during the trip at no charge. All calls home went over WhatsApp on the travel data line.

## Edge Cases That Break Travel eSIMs

**Hotspot restrictions.** Some plans block tethering entirely or cap it at a low volume. A laptop user who discovers this on arrival has no workaround except buying another plan.

**Mainland China.** Data routed through a foreign operator's core network does not pass through Chinese internet filtering, so Google services, WhatsApp and Western social media typically work over roaming eSIMs. Local Chinese SIM cards and local-breakout plans are subject to the same restrictions as domestic users.

**Identity registration.** Some countries require passport details for local mobile lines. Local prepaid eSIMs in those markets ask for document uploads during purchase, and processing can take several hours. Roaming-based travel eSIMs usually avoid this step.

**Changing or losing the phone mid-trip.** eSIM transfer between devices is supported for some carriers on newer iPhones and Android phones, but most travel eSIM profiles cannot be moved. If the phone is lost or replaced, the plan is gone unless the provider reissues the profile.

**Factory reset.** Both iOS and Android ask whether to keep or erase eSIM profiles during a reset. Choosing "erase" deletes the travel plan permanently with many providers.

## FAQs

### Can I use a travel eSIM and my regular SIM at the same time?

Yes, if the phone supports Dual SIM Dual Standby. The usual setup assigns mobile data to the travel eSIM and keeps the home SIM active for calls and SMS. Both lines stay registered on networks at once, but only one handles mobile data.

### Will I still receive SMS verification codes from my bank while abroad?

Yes, as long as the home line is switched on and international roaming is enabled at the carrier level. Data roaming on the home line can stay off. Incoming SMS is free with most carriers, but confirm this with yours, especially outside the EU.

### When should I install a travel eSIM?

One to three days before departure, at home, on Wi-Fi. If the plan's validity starts on installation rather than on first connection abroad, install it the evening before the flight to avoid losing paid days.

### Is an unlimited eSIM plan really unlimited?

Rarely in the full sense. Most unlimited plans provide full speed up to a daily threshold, often 1 to 5 GB, and then throttle the connection to a speed that supports messaging but not video. Hotspot use may also be limited.

### What happens if I delete the eSIM profile by mistake?

With many providers, the profile cannot be reinstalled from the same QR code, and the plan is lost. Some providers reissue profiles on request through support. Keep the QR code and manual activation codes stored outside the phone in case a reissue is possible.

### Does a travel eSIM come with a local phone number?

Most travel eSIMs are data-only and have no number that can receive calls or SMS. Some plans include a local number at a higher price. For most travelers, messaging apps and the home number cover communication needs.

## Conclusion

Choosing a travel eSIM comes down to matching five factors to the trip rather than to the advertised price. Coverage depends on the named partner network and its technology generation, not on the country list. The pricing model should fit how predictable your usage is: bundles for accurate planners, unlimited passes for heavy users, usage-based billing for irregular travel and uncertain needs. The validity start rule decides when to install. Support quality is worth testing with a pre-sale question before money changes hands. The home number stays reachable through dual-line setup, carrier-level roaming and a few settings changes made before departure. A traveler who checks these points at home, on Wi-Fi, with a day or two to spare, lands with a working connection and no surprises on the next bill.
